Start with a transcript, not the recording
A recording is hard to scan. You need a transcript: a word-for-word written version of everything said in the meeting.
Open your meeting recording. Look for a button or menu item called Transcript, Captions, or Subtitles. Download or copy the text. If your tool did not make one, use a speech-to-text tool: software that turns spoken audio into written text.
A transcript works like class notes. You do not re-watch the whole lesson to find one date; you read the notes instead.
Reference: [Speech-to-text guide](https://platform.openai.com/docs/guides/speech-to-text)

Paste the transcript into an AI chat
Open an AI chat: a website or app where you type messages to an AI that reads and writes text. Paste your transcript. Then give it a fixed structure so the output looks like real minutes.
Use this prompt:
You are an assistant that writes meeting minutes. Turn the transcript below into minutes with these sections:
1. Date and attendees
2. Decisions made
3. Action items, with owner and deadline
4. Next steps
Keep each section under four bullet points. Use plain language. If something is unclear, mark it with [unclear].
Transcript:
[PASTE YOUR TRANSCRIPT HERE]
The AI acts like a fast assistant who was not in the room. It writes quickly, but it does not know what mattered unless you tell it.

Check the output before you send
Read the minutes carefully. An AI can hallucinate: state something as fact that was never said. Check names, decisions, and deadlines against the transcript. Fix any mistakes. Then copy the minutes into your email or team chat and send them.

Expected result
You have a short set of minutes with decisions, owners, and deadlines. Paste them into an email or chat and send them within ten minutes, without re-listening to the recording.
