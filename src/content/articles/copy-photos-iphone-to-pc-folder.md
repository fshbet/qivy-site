Your phone is filling up with photos. If they live only on the phone, a lost or broken handset means losing them. Copying them to a PC folder takes about ten minutes and needs no extra apps.

## Plug your phone in and tell it to trust your PC

1. Unlock your iPhone.
2. Plug it into your PC with the charging cable.
3. On the iPhone, tap Trust when the message appears. This tells the phone it is safe to share data with this computer.
4. If the iPhone asks for your passcode, enter it.

Reference: [Transfer photos and videos from your iPhone, iPad, or iPod touch to your Mac or PC](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT201310)

## Open the photo folder on your phone

1. On your PC, open File Explorer. This is the app Windows uses to show files and folders.
2. Click your iPhone in the sidebar or under This PC.
3. Open Internal Storage, then open the folder called DCIM. This is where your phone keeps photos, like prints kept in a shoebox.
4. Open the subfolder, such as 100APPLE, that holds your pictures.

Reference: [Transfer photos and videos from your iPhone, iPad, or iPod touch to your Mac or PC](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT201310)

## Copy the photos to a folder on your PC

1. On your PC, open the Pictures folder.
2. Create a new folder, for example Phone photos.
3. Select the photos in the DCIM subfolder. Press Ctrl + C to copy them.
4. Open your new Phone photos folder. Press Ctrl + V to paste them.
5. Wait until the file-copy window finishes. Do not unplug the phone while files are moving.

### Expected result

Your PC folder now contains the same photos that were on your iPhone. You can open them, edit them, or back them up like any other file on your computer.

## Keep the copy safe

Copying the photos is only the first step. Keep the PC folder in your normal backup routine, or copy it to an external drive. If you delete the photos from your phone later, the PC folder still has them.

Ten minutes now means your photos are not trapped on one device.
