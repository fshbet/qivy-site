Many people install extensions—small add-ons that change what your browser can do—to read pages without ads, float video over other windows, or save screenshots. Firefox already has tools for these jobs. Using them means fewer extensions to manage and less data shared with add-on makers.

## Read an article without ads or sidebars
Reader View is a browser mode that shows only the main text and images of a page. It hides everything else. It is like tearing out a single article from a newspaper and throwing away the rest.

1. Open an article in Firefox.
2. Press F9, or click the page icon at the right side of the address bar.
3. Use the controls on the right to change the text size, font or background colour.

Expected result: The page shows clean text and images. Ads, menus and sidebars disappear.

Reference: [Firefox Reader View](https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/kb/firefox-reader-view)

## Keep playing video while you browse other tabs
Picture-in-Picture is a small video window that stays on top of other windows. You can move it anywhere on your screen.

1. Open a video in Firefox.
2. Right-click on the video, then right-click again on the menu that appears.
3. Choose Picture in Picture.

Expected result: A small video window floats above your other tabs and apps. The video keeps playing when you switch tabs.

Reference: [Picture-in-Picture](https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/kb/picture-picture)

## Save a page or part of a page as an image
A screenshot is an image of what is on your screen. Firefox can capture the whole page, the visible part, or an area you choose.

1. Right-click on an empty part of the page.
2. Choose Take Screenshot.
3. Select Save full page, Save visible, or drag to choose an area.
4. Click Download.

Expected result: The image saves to your downloads folder. You did not need an extension.

Reference: [Take screenshots in Firefox](https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/kb/take-screenshots-firefox)

These features are built into Firefox, so they update with the browser and do not need separate logins. Chrome, Edge and Safari offer similar tools; check their help pages for the steps.
