Your Wi-Fi shows full bars but nothing loads. Your USB drive worked yesterday and now the computer does not see it. An app freezes and will not close. These are three common 'it just stopped working' moments. None of them need a technician. Try these fixes in the next ten minutes.

## Your Wi-Fi says connected but nothing loads
1. Switch off your router or modem at the wall. Wait thirty seconds, then switch it back on. This clears a stuck connection without changing any settings.
2. Turn Wi-Fi off on your phone or laptop. Wait ten seconds, then turn it back on.
3. If pages still do not load, forget the network and reconnect. On a phone, tap the network name and choose Forget. On a computer, open Wi-Fi settings, remove the network, then enter the password again.
4. Open one website in a private browsing window. This window ignores saved cookies and site data. If the page loads, a browser extension or old cookie is likely the cause, not your internet connection.

Reference: [If your Mac doesn't connect to Wi-Fi networks](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT204110)

## A USB drive or external disk disappeared
1. Unplug the drive, wait five seconds, and plug it into a different port. Connect directly to the computer, not through a USB hub.
2. Open your file manager. On Windows, look under This PC in File Explorer. On a Mac, look under Locations in Finder.
3. If the drive is still missing, open Disk Management on Windows or Disk Utility on a Mac. If you see the disk listed but without a name or drive letter — the label Windows gives a disk, such as D: or E: — select it and choose Mount. On a Mac, mounting makes the disk available to the system.
4. Do not choose Erase, Format or Initialise unless you have copied the files elsewhere or no longer need them. That deletes everything on the disk.

Reference: [If a USB device isn't working with your Mac](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT201345)

## An app froze and will not close
1. Wait thirty seconds before doing anything. The app may recover on its own.
2. On Windows, press Control, Shift and Esc together to open Task Manager, the tool that shows running programs and lets you close them. Select the frozen app and click End task.
3. On a Mac, press Command, Option and Esc together. Select the app and click Force Quit. This tells the computer to close an app that is not responding.
4. Reopen the app. If it freezes again, look for an update in the app store or on the developer’s website, then restart your computer.

Reference: [If an app doesn't respond on your Mac](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT201285)

## Expected results
- Wi-Fi: websites load after the router restarts and you reconnect to the network.
- USB: the drive appears in File Explorer or Finder and you can open files.
- App: the frozen app closes and reopens normally.

If none of these work, the cause is likely a faulty router, a damaged cable, or software that needs reinstalling. Contact the device maker’s support next.
